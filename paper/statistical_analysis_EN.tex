\section{Inferential Statistical Analysis}
\label{sec:global-statistical-analysis}

Because metaheuristic outcomes are stochastic, normality was not assumed without prior verification. The three experimental domains were therefore analysed using a common non-parametric protocol based on 31 runs per method, instance, or function, with corresponding executions treated as paired under the same randomization conditions.

Normality was assessed with the Shapiro--Wilk test at $\alpha=0.05$. Paired
comparisons between the hybrid framework and each standalone metaheuristic used
the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Whenever multiple pairwise comparisons were
performed in the MKP and CEC2022 campaigns, $p$-values were adjusted using the
Holm step-down procedure. For HRES2--H2, the archived analysis reports
unadjusted pairwise $p$-values, as specified in its dedicated subsection below.
The Friedman test was used for omnibus comparisons and mean-rank calculations.
A result was considered statistically significant at $\alpha=0.05$ according
to the procedure stated for each campaign.

\subsection{Multidimensional Knapsack Problem (MKP)}
\label{sec:statistical-mkp-en}

The Shapiro--Wilk test rejected normality in 14 of the 36
instance--configuration distributions. Inference was therefore based on the
normalized mean percentage gap. The Friedman test detected differences among
the four framework configurations ($\chi_F^2=24.067$,
$p=2.42\times10^{-5}$). Mean ranks were 1.22 for DTW--3K, 1.78 for DDTW--3K,
3.11 for DTW--1K, and 3.89 for DDTW--1K; lower ranks indicate smaller gaps.

All six pairwise configuration contrasts remained significant after Holm
adjustment ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}\leq0.0391$). Increasing the iteration budget
reduced the average gap from 2.237\% to 1.805\% for DTW, a 19.3\% relative
reduction, and from 2.391\% to 1.876\% for DDTW, a 21.5\% relative reduction.

At the instance level, Friedman tests detected differences between the hybrid
framework and the seven standalone metaheuristics in all 36 campaigns
($36/36$, $p<0.05$). Across the 63 Holm-adjusted comparisons in each campaign,
the framework win/tie/loss records were $41/19/3$ for DTW--1K, $40/16/7$ for
DDTW--1K, $41/15/7$ for DTW--3K, and $40/15/8$ for DDTW--3K. These results
support portfolio robustness rather than universal dominance: GA achieved a
significant advantage in three DTW--1K instances, three DTW--3K instances,
three DDTW--1K instances, and four DDTW--3K instances.

Based on these results, the configuration yielding the lowest mean gap was
selected for each instance: DTW--3K for mknapcb1 and mknapcb3--mknapcb8, and
DDTW--3K for mknapcb2 and mknapcb9. This selection criterion is purely
descriptive and should not be interpreted as an additional pairwise
significance claim for every instance.

present the instance-level Friedman rankings and paired Wilcoxon tests for each
of the four MKP framework configurations. In each table, the control instance
is mknapcb1\_inst00 and the Wilcoxon direction is relative to that control.
Because the instances differ in objective magnitude, these tables serve as
supplementary detail; the normalized-gap analysis presented above constitutes
the primary inferential comparison among configurations.

\subsection{IEEE CEC2022 Continuous Benchmark}
\label{sec:statistical-cec-en}

Normality was rejected for the hybrid distribution in 9 of the 12 functions
for each alignment modality. After Holm adjustment of the five paired baseline
comparisons within each function, DTW achieved a win/tie/loss record of
$31/22/7$ across 60 comparisons and a mean within-function rank of 2.64.
DDTW achieved $32/23/5$ and a mean rank of 2.61.

The clearest advantage for both modalities occurred against WOA, for which each
framework recorded ten significant wins, two ties, and no losses. PSO, GWO, EHO,
and ACO remained competitive on selected functions; consequently, the
aggregate evidence supports robustness and competitiveness rather than
unconditional superiority.

Although DDTW obtained a lower mean objective than DTW in 10 of the 12
functions, the paired DTW--DDTW comparison produced no significant contrast
after Holm correction ($0/12$ significant comparisons). The available evidence
therefore does not establish one alignment modality as statistically superior
across the CEC2022 suite.

The Friedman test on raw objective values across the 12 functions yielded
$\chi_F^2=319.8635$ ($p=5.67\times10^{-62}$) for DTW and
$\chi_F^2=321.3226$ ($p=2.79\times10^{-62}$) for DDTW. However, because the
CEC2022 functions differ in bias and objective scale, these omnibus statistics
merely confirm heterogeneity across functions and do not directly rank
algorithm quality; the within-function paired comparisons reported above
provide the primary inferential evidence.

Tables~\ref{tab:cec-global-dtw-en} and~\ref{tab:cec-global-ddtw-en} present
the full cross-function Friedman rankings and paired Wilcoxon results for both
alignment modalities. The Wilcoxon direction in these tables is relative to the
CEC~1 control and should not be read as a direct quality ranking among
functions of different objective scales.

\subsection{HRES2--H2 Engineering Case}
\label{sec:statistical-hres2-en}

The HRES2--H2 experiment evaluates the sizing and rule-based dispatch of a
grid-connected wind--photovoltaic--electrolyzer--battery (WPEB) system. The
current implementation calculates annual hydrogen production from the
electrolyzer load, but it does not model a minimum hydrogen-demand target, a
hydrogen storage tank, or a fuel-cell conversion subsystem. The case study is
therefore interpreted as WPEB sizing with annual hydrogen production.

Each method was evaluated in 31 stochastic optimization runs with a maximum budget of 1,000 iterations, and each objective evaluation simulates 8,760 hourly dispatch steps. The benchmark uses one fixed synthetic weather profile for all runs, and corresponding executions were treated as paired for the inferential comparison. The HRES2--H2 pairwise values reported below are unadjusted $p$-values; no Holm correction is applied.

The Shapiro--Wilk test rejected normality for both hybrid LCOE distributions
($p<10^{-11}$). Omnibus Friedman tests detected differences among methods for
both DTW ($\chi_F^2=129.166$, $p=6.91\times10^{-23}$) and DDTW
($\chi_F^2=123.474$, $p=9.97\times10^{-22}$). Mean ranks were 1.71 for DTW
and 1.95 for DDTW.

Using the unadjusted Wilcoxon tests, all ten standalone metaheuristics had
$p<0.001$ against the corresponding proposed control and a higher mean rank,
yielding a $10/0/0$ win/tie/loss record at the unadjusted $\alpha=0.05$ level.
The independent Mann--Whitney sensitivity test also returned $p<0.05$ for all
ten baseline comparisons in both variants. These results support the observed
LCOE advantage on this fixed engineering scenario, without claiming
multiplicity-adjusted inference.

The direct paired comparison between DTW and DDTW found no significant LCOE
difference (Wilcoxon $p=0.593$). The observed difference in archived means was
only $7.42\times10^{-7}$ CNY/kWh, below the six-decimal reporting precision.
Therefore, the available evidence supports comparable economic performance of
the two alignment modalities rather than a meaningful economic advantage of
one modality over the other.

Because all methods in this engineering case share the same LCOE scale, the
Friedman and unadjusted Wilcoxon results can be compared directly without
normalization.

Tables~\ref{tab:hres2-pvalues-dtw-en} and~\ref{tab:hres2-pvalues-ddtw-en}
present the complete inferential results for the HRES2--H2 case. The control
in both tables is the corresponding hybrid framework; therefore, a ``Worse''
result indicates a higher (less desirable) LCOE than the hybrid control. The
pairwise $p$-values are unadjusted.

\subsection{Inferential Synthesis}
\label{sec:statistical-synthesis-en}

All three domains show statistically detectable global structure, but the
interpretation depends on the objective and scale of each problem. In MKP, the
3,000-iteration budget significantly reduces the gap and DTW--3K obtains the
best global rank. In CEC2022, DTW and DDTW are competitive variants with no
significant difference between them. In HRES2--H2, both variants significantly
outperform the standalone methods under the unadjusted pairwise comparisons,
and no significant difference was detected between them. The overall evidence
therefore supports framework robustness and stability, without implying
universal dominance on every problem or function.
